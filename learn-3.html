SIP & Mutual Funds

The simplest way for most people to build long-term wealth.

What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from thousands of investors and invests it in a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other assets — managed by a professional fund manager. You buy units of the fund, and your returns depend on how the portfolio performs.

What is a SIP?

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount regularly (monthly/weekly) in a mutual fund — like an EMI, but for growing your wealth. You invest ₹1,000/month, and the SIP auto-deducts it and buys fund units at the current price.

Why SIP beats lump sum for beginners

  • Rupee cost averaging: You buy more units when prices are low, fewer when high — averaging out your cost over time
  • No need to time the market: Consistency matters more than timing
  • Builds investing discipline: It's automatic, so you don't overthink it
  • Compounding works over time: Small amounts grow massively over 10–20 years
📊 The power of compounding: ₹5,000/month SIP at 12% returns for 20 years = ₹49.9 lakhs from just ₹12 lakh invested. Use our SIP calculator to run your own numbers.

Types of Mutual Funds

  • Equity funds — invest in stocks. High risk, high return. Best for 5+ year goals.
  • Debt funds — invest in bonds and government securities. Lower risk, stable returns (6–8%).
  • Hybrid funds — mix of equity and debt. Balanced risk-reward.
  • Index funds — track an index like Nifty 50. Low cost, no active management. Great for beginners.
  • ELSS — Equity Linked Savings Scheme. Invests in stocks AND saves tax under 80C.

How to start a SIP

  1. Complete KYC (once, using Aadhaar + PAN)
  2. Choose a platform: Groww, Zerodha Coin, Paytm Money, or directly via AMC website
  3. Pick a fund: for beginners, start with a Nifty 50 Index Fund
  4. Set your SIP amount and date (1–5 days after salary credit)
  5. Don't stop or pause during market dips — that's when SIP works hardest

Key terms to know

  • NAV — Net Asset Value; the price of one unit of a fund
  • Expense ratio — annual fee charged by the fund. Lower is better. Index funds: 0.1–0.2%, active funds: 0.5–2%
  • Exit load — small fee for redeeming within 1 year. Usually 1%.
  • CAGR — Compounded Annual Growth Rate; the true annualized return
