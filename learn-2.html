Emergency Fund

Your financial safety net — the foundation of every solid money plan.

What is an emergency fund?

An emergency fund is a dedicated pile of cash set aside for unexpected expenses — job loss, medical bills, urgent car repairs, or any financial shock that life throws at you. It's not an investment. It's insurance for your finances.

Why it's the #1 priority

Before you invest a single rupee, you need an emergency fund. Here's why: without it, any unexpected expense forces you to take on debt (credit card, personal loan) or liquidate investments at the wrong time.

3–6
Months of expenses is the standard target
6–12
Months recommended if you're self-employed or freelance

How much do you need?

Calculate your monthly essential expenses (rent + food + utilities + EMIs + insurance). Multiply by your target months.

Example: If your monthly essentials are ₹30,000, a 6-month emergency fund = ₹1,80,000. Keep this separate from your regular savings account.

Where to keep your emergency fund

The key is liquidity + safety. You need to access it in hours, not days. It should not be in stocks, mutual funds, or locked deposits.

  • High-yield savings account — best combo of accessibility and returns (4–7%)
  • Liquid mutual funds — slightly better returns, redeemable in 1 day (T+1)
  • FD with sweep-in facility — earns FD rates, accessible like savings account
  • Avoid: Stocks, equity MFs, crypto, real estate, long-term FDs

How to build it fast

  1. Open a separate savings account specifically for this — don't mix it with daily spending money
  2. Start with a mini emergency fund of ₹10,000–₹25,000 while you pay off high-interest debt
  3. Auto-transfer a fixed amount on salary day until the goal is hit
  4. Use windfalls (bonus, tax refund, gifts) to fast-track it

When to use it (and replenish)

Only for true emergencies: sudden medical bills, job loss, urgent home/vehicle repair. Not for vacations, sales, or planned expenses. When you do use it, replenish it immediately — treat it like a loan to yourself.

🔑 Rule of thumb: If you'd feel guilty spending the money, it's probably not an emergency. If you feel relieved, it was.
