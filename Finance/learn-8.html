Insurance Basics

Protection before accumulation — the financial principle most people get backwards.

Why insurance comes before investing

One medical emergency or early death can wipe out years of savings. Insurance transfers that catastrophic risk to an insurer for a small, predictable premium. It's not an investment — it's protection. Buy it for the coverage, not the returns.

Term Life Insurance

The purest and most important form of life insurance. You pay a premium; if you die during the policy term, your family receives the sum assured. No maturity benefit if you survive — that's fine, the goal is protection, not returns.

  • Who needs it: Anyone with financial dependants (spouse, children, parents)
  • How much: 10–15x your annual income, or enough to replace your income for 10–15 years
  • Term: Until age 60–65, or until your dependants are financially independent
  • Cost: Very affordable — ₹1 crore cover for a 30-year-old costs ~₹8,000–12,000/year
⚠️ Avoid: ULIPs, endowment plans, and money-back policies — these mix insurance with investment and do both poorly. Buy term insurance + separate mutual fund investment instead.

Health Insurance

Covers hospitalization costs — room, surgery, medicine, doctor fees. With healthcare costs rising 10–15%/year, even one hospitalization can cost ₹2–10 lakh. Health insurance is non-negotiable.

  • Individual policy: For yourself, if you're young and unmarried
  • Family floater: One policy covers entire family — cheaper than separate policies
  • Coverage amount: Minimum ₹5 lakh, ideally ₹10–20 lakh in a metro city
  • Buy early: Premiums are much lower in your 20s–30s, and pre-existing diseases are excluded for 2–4 years

What to check in a health policy

  • Waiting period for pre-existing conditions (lower is better)
  • Room rent limit (avoid policies that cap room rent — go for no-room-rent-limit)
  • Network hospitals in your city
  • Claim settlement ratio of the insurer (check IRDAI website — aim for 95%+)
  • No-claim bonus — sum insured increases each claim-free year

Vehicle Insurance

Third-party vehicle insurance is legally mandatory in India. Comprehensive cover (which also covers your own vehicle damage) is strongly recommended.

What you probably don't need

  • Credit card insurance / payment protection plans (expensive, rarely worth it)
  • Travel insurance for domestic trips (usually not worth the premium)
  • Child plans from LIC/insurance companies (buy term + ELSS instead)
