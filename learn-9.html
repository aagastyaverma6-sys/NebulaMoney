Retirement Planning

Start early. The math rewards patience unlike anything else.

Why start now?

The single biggest factor in retirement wealth isn't how much you earn — it's how early you start. Thanks to compounding, ₹5,000/month from age 25 grows to vastly more than ₹15,000/month starting at age 40, even though the total invested is less.

25x
Annual expenses is the retirement corpus target (4% rule)
30 yrs
Starting at 25 gives you 35 years vs starting at 40 (20 years)

How much do you need?

Use the 4% rule: you can safely withdraw 4% of your corpus annually without depleting it. So if you need ₹60,000/month (₹7.2 lakh/year), you need a corpus of ₹7.2L ÷ 4% = ₹1.8 crore — but adjusted for inflation at retirement time.

EPF — Employee Provident Fund

  • If you're salaried, 12% of basic salary is deducted + employer contributes 12%
  • Current interest rate: ~8.25% (tax-free)
  • Withdrawable at retirement or after 5 years of service (tax-free after 5 years)
  • Don't withdraw EPF when switching jobs — let it compound

PPF — Public Provident Fund

  • Government-backed, currently 7.1% interest (tax-free)
  • 15-year lock-in (extendable in 5-year blocks)
  • Max ₹1.5 lakh/year investment — qualifies for 80C deduction
  • Completely exempt from tax — invest, interest, and withdrawal all tax-free (EEE status)

NPS — National Pension System

  • Market-linked pension scheme with equity and debt allocation
  • Additional ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B) — over and above 80C
  • At 60: 60% lump sum (tax-free), 40% must be used to buy annuity (monthly pension)
  • Good for disciplined long-term saving — partially illiquid until 60
💡 Recommended order: Build emergency fund → Buy term + health insurance → Invest in ELSS/equity MFs → Contribute to PPF/NPS for retirement → Then explore advanced options.

Building a retirement portfolio

  • Age 20s–30s: 70–80% equity (high growth phase), 20–30% debt
  • Age 40s: 60% equity, 40% debt (start reducing risk)
  • Age 50s: 40% equity, 60% debt (protect what you've built)
  • At retirement: Primarily debt/annuity for stable income

FIRE — Financial Independence, Retire Early

A movement focused on extreme saving (50–70% of income) to retire in your 30s–40s. The math still uses the 25x rule, but the corpus must be larger since your retirement is longer. A goal worth knowing about even if you don't pursue it fully.
