Tax Planning

Pay only what you owe — and not a rupee more.

New vs Old Tax Regime (India)

India now has two income tax regimes. You choose one each year when filing your ITR.

  • New regime — lower slab rates, but most deductions removed. Simpler. Better if you don't have many investments.
  • Old regime — higher slab rates, but allows deductions (80C, HRA, home loan interest, etc.). Better if you invest and claim deductions.

New Tax Regime Slabs (FY 2024–25)

  • Up to ₹3 lakh — Nil
  • ₹3–7 lakh — 5%
  • ₹7–10 lakh — 10%
  • ₹10–12 lakh — 15%
  • ₹12–15 lakh — 20%
  • Above ₹15 lakh — 30%
💡 Rebate: Under the new regime, income up to ₹7 lakh is effectively tax-free due to the rebate under Section 87A.

Key deductions under Old Regime

  • Section 80C (₹1.5 lakh limit) — ELSS funds, PPF, EPF, life insurance premium, home loan principal, NSC, 5-year FD
  • Section 80D — health insurance premium (₹25,000 self + ₹50,000 for parents)
  • HRA exemption — if you pay rent, a portion of your HRA is tax-free
  • Section 24(b) — home loan interest up to ₹2 lakh
  • Standard deduction — ₹50,000 flat for salaried employees
  • Section 80CCD(1B) — additional ₹50,000 for NPS contribution

When is the Old Regime better?

If your total deductions exceed ~₹3.75 lakh (the standard deduction + 80C + 80D + NPS), the old regime saves more tax. Run the numbers both ways using our Tax Calculator.

Smart tax-saving moves

  1. Invest in ELSS (mutual funds that save tax under 80C — only 3-year lock-in vs 15 years for PPF)
  2. Buy a health insurance policy for yourself and parents (80D)
  3. Contribute to NPS for extra ₹50,000 deduction (80CCD 1B)
  4. File ITR even if no tax is due — builds financial record, needed for loans and visas
  5. Plan tax-loss harvesting in equity — sell loss-making stocks to offset gains

ITR Filing deadlines

  • Individuals (non-audit): 31 July of the assessment year
  • With audit: 31 October
  • Belated return: 31 December (with late fee of ₹1,000–₹5,000)
⚠️ Important: Tax planning is legal. Tax evasion is illegal. The goal is to legally reduce your taxable income using government-approved instruments — not to hide income.
