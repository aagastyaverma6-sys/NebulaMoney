Budgeting Basics

Learn how to create a budget that actually works — and stick to it.

What is a budget?

A budget is simply a plan for your money. It tells every rupee where to go before the month begins. Without a budget, money disappears — you earn, you spend, and wonder where it all went. With a budget, you're in control.

The 50/30/20 Rule

The most popular budgeting framework, popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren. Divide your after-tax income into three buckets:

50%
Needs — rent, food, bills, EMIs
30%
Wants — dining, shopping, travel
20%
Savings & investments
💡 Example: If you earn ₹50,000/month after tax — spend max ₹25,000 on needs, ₹15,000 on wants, and save/invest at least ₹10,000.

Zero-Based Budgeting

A stricter approach: assign every rupee a job so Income − Expenses = ₹0. You're not spending everything — some jobs are saving and investing. This forces intentionality.

  • List all income sources for the month
  • List every expense, including irregular ones (annual subscriptions, birthdays)
  • Assign savings and investments as non-negotiable "expenses"
  • Adjust until the difference is zero

Envelope Method

A classic cash-based system. Divide physical cash (or digital budget categories) into envelopes — Food, Transport, Entertainment, etc. When an envelope is empty, spending stops for that category.

How to build your first budget

  1. Track 1 month of spending first — use your Expenses page to see where money actually goes
  2. Identify fixed vs variable expenses — rent is fixed; food varies
  3. Set realistic category limits — don't cut too aggressively, you'll quit
  4. Automate savings — set a recurring transfer on salary day
  5. Review monthly — adjust as life changes
🔑 Key insight: The best budget is the one you'll actually follow. A rough budget you stick to beats a perfect budget you abandon after two weeks.

Common budgeting mistakes

  • Forgetting irregular expenses (car service, festivals, trips)
  • Setting budgets too tight — you'll always "fail"
  • Not tracking for at least 2–3 months before drawing conclusions
  • Budgeting separately from your partner (if applicable)
