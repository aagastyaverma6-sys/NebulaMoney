Inflation & Purchasing Power

Your money is losing value right now. Here's what to do about it.

What is inflation?

Inflation is the rate at which the general price level of goods and services rises over time. If inflation is 6%, something that cost ₹100 today will cost ₹106 next year. Your ₹100 now buys less than it did before — that's purchasing power erosion.

At 6% inflation, prices double in ~12 years (Rule of 72)

The Rule of 72

A quick mental math shortcut: divide 72 by any rate to find how long it takes to double (or halve). At 6% inflation, ₹100 becomes ₹50 in purchasing power in 72÷6 = 12 years. At 12% investment returns, money doubles in 72÷12 = 6 years.

Real vs Nominal Returns

If your FD gives 7% and inflation is 6%, your real return is only ~1%. You're barely staying ahead. This is why putting all your savings in a savings account (3–4%) is actually losing you money in real terms.

  • Nominal return — what the investment says it returns (7% FD)
  • Real return — nominal − inflation (7% − 6% = 1% real)
  • Inflation-adjusted return — your true purchasing power gain
🧮 Formula: Real Return ≈ Nominal Return − Inflation Rate. More precisely: (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1

How different assets perform against inflation

  • Equity / Stocks — historically returns 12–15% in India, strongly beats 6% inflation ✅
  • Real estate — appreciates 8–12% long term, beats inflation ✅
  • Gold — tracks inflation over very long periods, roughly neutral 🟡
  • FD / RD — 6.5–7.5%. Barely beats inflation, before tax ❌
  • Savings account — 3–4%. Loses to inflation badly ❌
  • Under the mattress — 0%. Loses 6% of value every year ❌❌

Strategies to beat inflation

  1. Keep only your emergency fund in low-risk accounts — invest the rest
  2. Allocate a significant portion to equity (stocks, equity mutual funds) for long-term goals
  3. Include inflation-indexed bonds (RBI Floating Rate Bonds) in your debt portfolio
  4. Increase your SIP amount by at least 10% every year to keep up with inflation
  5. Factor in inflation when planning for retirement — ₹50,000/month today ≠ ₹50,000/month in 20 years

Inflation and retirement planning

This is where inflation bites hardest. If you need ₹50,000/month today and you retire in 25 years, at 6% inflation you'll need about ₹2.15 lakh/month for the same lifestyle. Your retirement corpus must account for this.
